Desired proximity → colour

Colour states what the relationship asks for, not whether the layout delivers it. “Not far” is the same orange as “far”, drawn dotted.

Importance → line width & weight

Weights step by powers of four, so one critical relationship outranks sixty-three desirable ones. Vital relationships carry no weight — they are feasibility constraints, drawn at width 18 with a white X.

Layout geometry

Show relationships

Drag a center to move it, drag the square at its lower-right corner to resize, and drag a triangle to slide that station around its center's walls. A hollow triangle pointing inward is the input station; a solid one pointing outward is the output. The relationship table decides which stations exist: a center gets an output the moment some relationship measures from it and an input the moment one measures to it, and the station leaves again when the last of those relationships does. This table only sets where each one sits, and a position is remembered while unused. Traced from the course drawing — the block positions reproduce the centroid distances the course reports for B–D, B–C and C–D.

Satisfaction curves

One curve per desired proximity. Read a relationship's desired proximity from the table, take its measured distance along the bottom, and the curve gives the satisfaction that multiplies its weight. Adjacent through Not far fall as distance grows; Far and Very far rise. The dots are the distances and satisfactions the course case reports — a curve that misses them will not reproduce Layout 0's score.

Desired proximity Form Parameters (meters) Drawn as

A ramp runs between the distance where satisfaction is 1 and the distance where it is 0; bend curves it — 1 is a straight line, above 1 drops off faster at first, below 1 holds on longer. A bell peaks at its ideal distance and falls away on both sides, reaching half satisfaction one half-width out; it is the only form that can say “not too far, not too near”, where closer is not always better. The axis runs to 30 m — raise it for a larger facility.

Importance scale

Every level available in the table's Importance column. Rename one, change its weight or its drawn line width, or add a level of your own — the Importance dropdown and the maximum possible value update straight away. A level marked constraint carries no weight: it must be satisfied for the layout to be feasible, and is drawn with a white X.

Flow estimation matrix

Show empty trips

Enter the loaded trips per period from each center to every other — these come from the process and organization design, or from flow tracking. A loaded trip runs from the source center's output station to the destination's input station. Everything else on this page is computed: the empty trips that return each vehicle to its next pickup, and every margin total. The diagonal takes no input, because a center never ships a loaded trip to itself.

Shaded italics are empty trips, running the other way round — from the source center's input station to the destination's output station, because a vehicle that has just unloaded is standing at the input dock. They are placed so that every center balances, arrivals equalling departures, at the least total empty travel. The diagonal carries the cheapest move of all: unload and reload within the same center.
